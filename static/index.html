How do I make an iPhone backup? (short version)
  1. Connect the iPhone to this Mac with a cable. If the phone asks, tap Trust.
  2. Open Finder and click the iPhone in the sidebar (on older Macs, open iTunes).
  3. Choose “Back up all of the data on your iPhone to this Mac”.
  4. Tick “Encrypt local backup” and choose a password you will remember. Encrypted backups contain more information, so the check is more thorough.
  5. Click Back Up Now and wait until it finishes. Then click “look again”.

How do I create a bug report on the phone? (short version)
  1. Open Settings → About phone and tap Build number 7 times until it says you are a developer.
  2. Go to Settings → System → Developer options (the location can vary by brand).
  3. Tap Take bug report → Full report. Wait a few minutes for the notification.
  4. Tap the notification and send the .zip to this computer: by cable, by email to yourself, or with cloud storage.
  5. When you're done, you can turn Developer options off again.
What is AndroidQF?

AndroidQF is a free collection tool, also from Amnesty International's Security Lab. It runs with the phone connected to a computer and saves a folder of data. If a digital-security helpline helped you collect data, it is probably an AndroidQF folder. Choose that folder (or its .zip) below.

    What this scanner checks, and how

    Full transparency: below are the exact spyware families on the list, the kinds of traces they leave, and every check that runs on your data. It is all read live from the installed tools.

    How it works

    1. Copy. The phone's data is copied to this computer (iPhone backup, Android bug report or AndroidQF). The phone itself is not changed.
    2. Extract. MVT reads specific records from that copy: messages, browsing history, installed apps, network and battery logs, system diagnostics, and more (full list below).
    3. Compare. Every web address, program name, file, app and account it finds is compared with indicators of compromise: traces that researchers found on phones confirmed to be infected.
    4. Report. Any match is reported. No match means no known trace was found.
    What it cannot do. MVT doesn't test the phone for vulnerabilities or exploits, and it can't spot spyware nobody has documented yet. It looks for the aftermath of known attacks: the links, servers, process names and files that specific spyware left behind on other victims' phones. A clean result is good news, not a guarantee. Spyware sometimes erases its own traces.

    Preparing the phone: a step-by-step guide

    Read this before you connect the phone. It takes about 15 minutes, plus the time the backup needs. Nothing in this guide deletes anything from the phone.

    1 · Before you start

    You will need:

    1.1 · Don't change anything on the phone yet

    Spyware traces are hidden in ordinary records: messages, browser history, system logs. Until the check is done:

    • Don't factory-reset or wipe the phone.
    • Don't update the operating system.
    • Don't delete suspicious messages, links or apps.
    • Don't install “anti-spyware” apps from app stores.

    If you think someone is actively watching you, don't talk about your suspicion on or near this phone. Contact helpers from a different device.

    1.2 · Switch on Airplane Mode

    This cuts the phone off from the internet, so nobody can remotely send commands to the spyware (some spyware can erase itself) while you prepare.

    • iPhone: swipe down from the top-right corner and tap the airplane. Make sure Wi-Fi and Bluetooth are off too (on iPhone, tapping them in Control Centre only disconnects; turn them off in Settings).
    • Android: swipe down twice from the top and tap Airplane mode.

    Airplane Mode doesn't affect the cable connection. The backup works normally.

    1.3 · Optional: take out the SIM card

    For extra certainty you can also remove the SIM card. The phone then can't use the mobile network even if Airplane Mode gets switched off by mistake.

    1. Find the small tray on the side of the phone with a tiny hole next to it.
    2. Push the SIM tool (or a straightened paper clip) firmly into the hole. The tray slides out.
    3. Take the SIM out, put the empty tray back, and keep the SIM somewhere safe.

    Many new iPhones use an eSIM with no card to remove. Airplane Mode is enough there. When you put the SIM back later, the phone may ask for the SIM PIN.

    1.4 · Is it safe to connect a possibly infected phone to my computer?

    For most people, yes. The risk is very low. Digital-security helplines routinely have people back up possibly infected iPhones to their normal computer for exactly this check.

    • Known spyware targets phones, not computers. There is no public evidence that Pegasus, Predator or the other spyware on the list spreads from a phone to a computer over a cable.
    • Data flows one way. During a backup, the computer reads files from the phone through Apple's own backup system. The phone can't make the computer run anything.
    • The backup is just data: databases, settings and logs. Spyware traces inside it are records, not programs running on your computer.

    In theory, an attacker could use an unknown security hole in the computer's USB or backup handling. Such holes are rare and very expensive, and no case like this is known.

    Good habits:

    • Keep your computer's system updated.
    • At high risk? (e.g. journalists with sensitive sources on their computer, activists under active surveillance) If possible, use a spare computer or a separate user account just for this check, and delete the backup afterwards (see step 4).
    • Android: the bug report can be sent from the phone without any cable at all (step 3.3).

    2 · iPhone: make a backup on this Mac

    2.1 · Connect the iPhone with a cable

    Always use a wired connection. Wi-Fi syncing is slower, less reliable, and needs a network, which we just switched off.

    • Plug the cable into the Mac directly, not through a hub or a public charging station.
    • Unlock the iPhone and keep it unlocked while you start the backup.
    • Don't unplug it until the backup has finished.

    2.2 · Tap “Trust” on the iPhone

    The first time you connect, the iPhone asks “Trust This Computer?”. Tap Trust and enter the phone's passcode. This only allows this Mac to read the phone. You can undo it later (see step 4).

    No question appears? The phone may already trust this Mac. That's fine, continue.

    2.3 · Open Finder and set up the backup

    1. Open Finder and click your iPhone in the left sidebar under “Locations”. (On macOS before 10.15, open iTunes and click the small phone icon.)
    2. In the General tab, choose “Back up all of the data on your iPhone to this Mac”.
    3. Tick “Encrypt local backup” (strongly recommended, see 2.4).
    4. Click Back Up Now.

    2.4 · Encrypted or not? Choose encrypted

    Not encryptedEncrypted BEST
    Messages, photos, app data✓✓
    Browsing history, call history, Wi-Fi & network data, Health, saved passwords✗✓

    Protected if the backup file is lost✗✓

    When you tick “Encrypt local backup”, the Mac asks for a password:

    • Choose one you will remember and write it down. The scanner needs it, and Apple can't recover it.
    • This is not the phone's passcode or your Apple ID password. It's only for this backup.
    • If the box was already ticked, a password was set earlier. Use that one.
    I forgot the backup password

    On the iPhone go to Settings → General → Transfer or Reset iPhone → Reset → Reset All Settings. This removes the backup password without deleting your data, though settings such as Wi-Fi passwords, wallpaper and display settings return to their defaults. Then set a new password in Finder. Don't choose “Erase All Content and Settings”.

    2.5 · Wait for the backup to finish

    A progress bar appears in the Finder sidebar next to the iPhone. Depending on how much is on the phone, this takes 5 minutes to over an hour. Leave the cable plugged in and the Mac awake.

    It's done when Finder shows “Latest backup to this Mac: Today …”.

    Then open this scanner, choose iPhone, and the new backup appears in the list automatically.

    2.6 · Option: backup made on another computer

    You can make the backup on one Mac and check it on another, for example by bringing it on a USB stick. Use an encrypted backup for this: a stick is easily lost, and only an encrypted backup is protected by a password.

    On the computer with the backup:

    1. In Finder, click the iPhone, then Manage Backups…. Right-click the backup and choose Show in Finder.
    2. A folder with a long name like 00008110-001A2B3C… is highlighted. Copy that whole folder to the stick. Don't copy only its contents, and don't rename it.
    3. Eject the stick properly before unplugging it.

    On this computer: plug in the stick, open the scanner, choose iPhone → “the backup is somewhere else…” and select the folder with the long name. If macOS asks whether the scanner may access a removable volume, click Allow.

    • Stick format: exFAT or APFS. Avoid FAT32 (“MS-DOS”), which can't store files larger than 4 GB. You can check this in Disk Utility.
    • Space: the stick needs about as much free space as the backup (shown in Manage Backups…). This computer needs about the same again, because an encrypted backup is unlocked into a temporary copy that is deleted after the check.
    • Tip: copying the folder from the stick to this computer first makes the check faster.

    3 · Android: create a bug report

    Menu names differ slightly between brands (Samsung, Pixel, Xiaomi, …). Look for similar wording.

    3.1 · Unlock “Developer options”

    1. Open Settings → About phone. On some phones also tap Software information.
    2. Tap Build number seven times in a row.
    3. Enter your PIN if asked. A message says “You are now a developer!”

    This doesn't change anything on the phone. It only shows an extra settings menu. You can switch it off afterwards.

    3.2 · Take a full bug report

    1. Go to Settings → System → Developer options (Samsung: Settings → Developer options, at the very bottom).
    2. Tap Take bug report, choose Full report, then Report.
    3. Wait 2–10 minutes. Keep the phone on and don't switch it off.

    Airplane Mode can stay on. Creating the report doesn't need the internet.

    3.3 · Move the .zip file to this computer

    A notification “Bug report captured” appears. Tap it and choose how to send the file:

    • Cable (best): connect the phone, choose File transfer on the phone, and copy the .zip. On a Mac you may need the free “Android File Transfer” app, or use the share option below.
    • Email to yourself or a cloud drive. This needs Airplane Mode turned off briefly. Do it only after the report was created.

    The file is called something like bugreport-…-2026-11-09.zip. In the scanner choose Android → A bug report file.

    More thorough: AndroidQF (with a helper)

    AndroidQF collects much more than a bug report. It needs USB debugging turned on in Developer options and a cable connection, and it runs as a small program on the computer. If a digital-security helper is supporting you, they will usually do this part. Afterwards, choose Android → An AndroidQF collection in the scanner.

    4 · After the check

    • If the result is red: keep the phone as it is (no reset), keep the result folder and the backup, and contact a helpline (e.g. Access Now, accessnow.org/help).
    • If the result is clean: you can switch Airplane Mode off, put the SIM back, and update the phone.
    • The backup contains your private data. If you don't need it anymore, delete it: Finder → iPhone → Manage Backups… → select → Delete Backup. Moved it on a USB stick (step 2.6)? Delete it from the stick, from the other computer and from any copy on this one, then empty the Trash.
    • Android: switch Developer options off again (toggle at the top of that menu) and delete the bug report .zip from places you sent it to (email, cloud).
    • If this wasn't your own computer: on the iPhone you can remove the trust again with Settings → General → Transfer or Reset iPhone → Reset → Reset Location & Privacy.
